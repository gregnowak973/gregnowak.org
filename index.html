About

I've spent seven years at Google across Ads and Cloud, building the models, frameworks, and pipelines that turn ambiguous business questions into decisions leaders can act on. The titles have changed along the way, but the job hasn't: help businesses grow, and show the numbers behind it — whether that means launching a scaled GTM strategy or proving a new AI technology is worth betting on.

My background is deliberately a mix. Neuroscience at Princeton taught me to reason from data. Graduate CS coursework at NYU taught me to build. A Wharton private equity certificate added the capital-allocation lens. And outside work I'm usually shipping something — an automated trading system, a GCP-native SaaS — because the fastest way to understand a technology is to build with it.

The journey so far

2019 – Present

Google

Analytical Consultant → Principal → Head of Category Expansion

I joined Google to advise its largest advertisers on measurement and growth, and stayed because the problems kept getting bigger. Along the way I took a detour advising startups for CapitalG (Google's growth fund), won a global rotation to São Paulo to build a VC channel from scratch — fourteen workshops in six weeks, in a market I'd never worked, for founders I'd never met — and became the first IC Mastery Coach at Google, teaching other consultants how to do this work.

One arc I'm proud of: several years spent on AI value creation — figuring out where AI investments can drive real business objectives. I did that with startups in Brazil, PE-backed companies in the US, and US enterprises, where I led the go-to-market for a Gemini Enterprise video creative AI agent from pilot to launch.

Today Google has a role it created for me: Head of Category Expansion, showing CMOs and CEOs where their next markets are — and building the analytical engine that finds them. At Google I've done this mostly by driving growth with companies; now I want to get back to doing it in a more technical way.

2017 – 2019

Jet.com

Paid Search Lead → Growth Product Manager

I left academia for e-commerce hypergrowth and got a crash course in commercial reality. I started by running the P&L for paid-search categories, then moved into product — studying master's-level computer science at night and working with engineers to execute things like migrating the data systems behind half the company's sales to a big-data environment. Jet taught me that I could learn any technical domain fast enough to ship in it, and that I loved doing exactly that.

2015 – 2017

Princeton Neuroscience Institute

Research Specialist

My first job was helping a brand-new neuroscience lab get off the ground — designing experiments, building recording rigs, and teaching myself programming, CAD, and machining because someone had to. Studying decision-making under uncertainty turned out to be the perfect foundation for everything since: markets, marketing, and AI are all the same problem wearing different clothes.
